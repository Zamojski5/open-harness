# Composition

## Framing the subject
- Decide the **one focal point** first. Everything else supports it: lines lead to it, contrast peaks at it, sharpness lives there.
- **Thirds**: put the focal point on a third line or intersection; horizons on the upper or lower third, not the middle — unless symmetry is the idea (then make it exact).
- **Look room / lead room**: leave more space in front of a face or moving object than behind it.
- **Headroom**: in a medium shot eyes sit on the upper third line; don't cut at joints (neck, knees, wrists, ankles).
- **Negative space** is a design element: posters and product shots get more premium with 40-60% empty, quiet area — also gives room for type.

## Guiding the eye
- Leading lines (roads, rails, edges, light shafts) pointing into the subject.
- Frames within the frame (doorways, branches, arches).
- Depth layers: foreground element (out of focus or dark), mid-ground subject, background with atmosphere. Haze/fog separates layers in 3D for almost free.
- The eye goes to: brightest area, highest contrast, faces, text, warm saturated colour. Make sure those agree.
- Odd numbers (1, 3, 5) of repeated objects read more naturally than even.

## Shot sizes
Extreme wide (place/scale) → wide (subject in context) → medium (body language) → close-up (emotion, product detail) → extreme close-up (texture, tension). Sequences cut between sizes, not between two similar ones.

## Lens and camera (Blender focal length on 36 mm sensor ≈ full frame)
| Focal length | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 14-24 mm | Expansive, distorted near edges, exaggerated depth | Architecture, epic landscapes, dynamic low angles |
| 28-35 mm | Natural with context, documentary | Environment + character, walk-and-talk |
| 50 mm | Neutral, "human eye" | Default for mid shots |
| 85-135 mm | Compressed, flattering, background melts | Portraits, product hero shots, isolating a subject |
| 200 mm+ | Strong compression, stacked layers | Heat-haze, crowds, sun/moon looking huge |
- Depth of field: f/1.4-2.8 for isolation (product, portrait), f/8-11 for landscapes. In Blender set focus object, not distance, so animation stays sharp.
- Camera height: eye level = neutral; low angle = power/hero; high angle = vulnerability/overview; top-down = graphic, flat-lay.
- Dutch tilt only for unease, 5-15°, and sparingly.
- Keep verticals vertical for architecture and products (use shift instead of tilting the camera).

## Aspect ratios
16:9 video, 9:16 vertical short-form (keep key content in the middle 1080×1420 safe zone), 4:5 feed post, 1:1 square, 2.39:1 cinematic letterbox, 3:4 / 2:3 posters. Compose for the final crop from the start; if one master must serve several, keep the subject centred with generous margins.

## Product shots
Three-point light (key 45° side-above, soft fill opposite at ~1/4 power, rim behind for edge separation), seamless sweep background, contact shadow under the object, slight reflection for glossy goods. Hero angle: 3/4 view, camera slightly above, 85-100 mm.
