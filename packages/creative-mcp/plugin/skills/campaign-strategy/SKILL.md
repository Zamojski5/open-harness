# Campaign strategy

## Goal

Arrive at an idea that is true for the audience, ownable by the brand and built in a format the evidence supports, before anyone designs or generates anything. Strategy exists so that the creative team (you, the builders, the auditors) argue about an idea, not about taste. Good strategy is short: a page the user can read in two minutes and make a decision from.

## What great looks like

- The objective is measurable and the audience is a specific person in a specific moment, not a demographic.
- The insight is a tension you can point to evidence for (reviews, forum threads, search queries, references), stated in a sentence a human would say. The proposition follows from it and one big idea carries the proposition.
- There are two or three directions that are really different from each other: a different tone, format, image world and way of using the brand's system, not three rewrites of the same layout and colours.
- Each direction's format choice is justified by the pattern map from `reference-research` and names the performance signal behind it.
- Each comes with a mood board (`reference_collect`/`reference_board`) and one sample hook, so the user can feel it.
- The decision is made in the open: the user picks, or when running autonomously the director picks and says why in a few lines.

## How to get there

Research first, and use the `reference-research` skill for the video part. Add competitors (what they promise, how they look, what they all do the same, which is the opening for difference) and audience pains in their own words (WebSearch for reviews, communities, comments; quote them). Write down what you could not find rather than filling the gap with a plausible guess.

Then move from insight to proposition to the single big idea, and test it: could a competitor say it, does it still work with the logo removed, does it survive being shortened to a six second hook. Only then split into directions.

**Using the brand range.** A brand book lists constants; the pattern file's brand expression range shows what can change. Each direction states which variables it plays with (layout and composition, which colour pairing leads, image style, crop and scale, motion language, tone of copy) and which constants it holds. Directions that hold the same variables will look the same, so pick different ones on purpose. If the brief wants one consistent look, say that and why. Everything in the constants stays strict: logo use, type families, palette set, voice.

**Format, by what the idea needs.** Show people when emotion, trust or identification carries the message and a face makes the viewer feel it (parents, patients, creators, testimonials). Show only the app or product when the proof is the product doing something quickly and clearly, and the audience already knows the problem; real screens, never generated UI. Dramatise the problem when the pain is felt but unnamed and a sharp situation makes people say "that's me". Mix when the evidence supports it. Say which you chose and which signal supports it; the pattern map usually shows what the category over- and under-uses.

## Output

A strategy note (a page, usually in `production/strategy.md`): objective, audience, insight with evidence, proposition, big idea, then each direction with its format rationale, the variables it plays, mood board, sample hook and what it would cost or risk. End with the decision gate and record the choice with `project_brief decide`. Then hand the chosen direction to `copywriting` and `production-pipeline`.

## Pitfalls

- A big idea that is a slogan, or a direction that is only a different headline.
- Using the category's most common format because it is common (the pattern map is there to show where the room is).
- Treating the brand book as a template so every direction, and every deliverable in each, looks identical.
- Research that confirms the first idea, rather than challenging it.
