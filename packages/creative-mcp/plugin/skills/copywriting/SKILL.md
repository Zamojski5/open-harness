# Copywriting

## Goal

Words that say one clear, specific thing in the brand's voice and still work at the size and speed they will be read: three seconds, thumb-height, over a moving picture. Copy fails when it is generic enough to belong to any brand, or when every deliverable in the campaign repeats the same line.

## What great looks like

- A message hierarchy first: the one thing the viewer must take away, the proof or reason behind it, the action. Every piece of text serves one of these.
- A real spread of options per direction, as a rule of thumb ten to twenty headline, hook and CTA variants that differ in angle (benefit, curiosity, pain, social proof, humour, a number), not in word order. Keep the few that are strong; drop the rest openly.
- The brand voice, in the brand's language. Polish brands get native Polish, with natural rhythm and correct diacritics, not translated English. Read it aloud.
- Specific beats safe: a concrete number, name, moment or promise. A line that would still make sense under a competitor's logo is filler.
- Fit for the format: length against the safe areas and reading time of the platform (`media_review` and the OCR checks measure this), one idea per card, legible at phone size.
- Variety across the deliverables: different pieces carry different lines and angles unless the strategy deliberately repeats a signature line.

## How to get there

Start from the chosen direction and the audience's own words in the research (quote their phrasing, it is often better than ours). Write widely, then cut. For video, write the hook for the first three seconds separately from the body, and the CTA separately from both. Pair each line with what is on screen, since text and picture should not say the same thing twice.

Then let someone else judge. Send a reviewer subagent only the lines, the brief, the voice notes and the format limits (not your reasoning) and ask it to score each variant for clarity, specificity, brand voice, length and safe area for the format, and for whether it says something or is filler, with one sentence of evidence each. It should also look at the set as a whole: are the lines distinct from each other, and are any off-brand in tone? Keep what scores well, rewrite what does not, and say what changed. When the reviewer and you disagree, the user's brand voice rules decide.

**Performance variants.** For A/B, vary one thing at a time (hook angle, CTA, proof point) and name each variant after what it tests, so results are readable later. Prefer two or three real contrasts over many near-duplicates.

## Pitfalls

- Slogan-speak ("Discover the future of...") and superlatives with no proof.
- Copy written before the visual is known, then squeezed into the safe area.
- The same headline on every format of the campaign.
- Polish that reads as translated, missing diacritics, or invented words in the brand's name or product terms.

## Output

`production/copy.md`: the hierarchy, the chosen lines per deliverable with their variants and reviewer scores, and the A/B plan if asked.
